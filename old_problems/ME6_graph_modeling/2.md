# ME 6: Graph Modelling

Bob is practicing his yoga. There are $p$ positions he can be in. There is an adjacency matrix of positive, non-zero values `adj` that expresses the cost of moving from position `i` to position `j` as `adj[i][j]`. It is possible to move from any position to any *different* position, and it's cost will be at least `m`, which is the minimum value in the matrix. Bob's starting position is position 0. Bob cannot remain in a position.

There is no specific destination position, but Bob wants to do exactly `n` moves. Bob also has incredible will-power, and he may focus himself up to 3 times to reduce the cost of a move by half. 

Our goal is to treat this as a graph modelling problem, and write an algorithm to determine the minimum amount of total effort Bob can expend while completing his workout of `n` moves.

1. (25 pts) Define how you encode a minimal state to be put in the priority queue for Dijkstra's algorithm or A\*. That is, show me what variables go in the state. Include the metric in the state. Mark or underline the value that will be used to sort the queue.

2. (25 pts) Given a state, how do we derive every valid neighboring state? Show how every variable is modified from one state to the next.

3. (10 pts) Suppose we use `moves_remaining * m / 2.0` as our heuristic, where `m` is the smallest value of any element of `adj`. Is this consistent or not? Don't consider whether A\* even adds anything here.

4. (40 pts) Prove your answer for number 3. Include the definition for consistency for either yes or no proofs.
